06 / HOW TO READ THE RESULTS

Methods & caveats

A research interface over the saved peptide–HLA stability models. Every displayed prediction comes from a fitted model.

SCOPE

Retrospective research

Predicted peptide–HLA half-life for 9-mer, class-I pairs. The primary input is the HLA 34-residue contact pseudosequence. This is not an experimental or clinical validation.

UNCERTAINTY

Five-fit range

The range is the minimum–maximum across five peptide cross-validation fits. It is a sensitivity diagnostic, not a calibrated confidence interval. For a known peptide, four fits can include that peptide.

PREDICTION

Exposure and folds

A peptide present in the dataset uses the fit that held out that entire peptide, even for a new allele pairing. Completely new peptides use the mean of the five models on the log scale. Negative log predictions are clipped to zero for display.

MODELS

Five approaches

BLOSUM MLP is the supervised reference. BLOSUM Ridge and allele-ID Ridge are sequence controls. ESM-2 independent mean and joint sequence encoding use frozen protein representations with a Ridge head. Joint encoding does not imply a physical complex model.

DATA

Zeros and measurement coverage

Reported zeros are retained, although they may be left-censored. Engineered C67S constructs are excluded. Measurement counts describe this dataset, not human population frequencies. No replicate-level variance is available.

INTERPRETATION

Ranking is a starting point

Low: <2 hours. Intermediate: ≥2 and <6 hours. High: ≥6 hours. Scanning a full protein is an exploratory use beyond the measured peptide dataset. Predictions require experimental follow-up.

Training counts are available outer-fold rows; the MLP reserves 15% of training groups for early stopping.
